## Ulfberht

This site is inspired by the **Ulfberht**, a Viking-era blade stamped
+ULFBERHT+ along its fuller, forged between the 9th and 11th centuries.
A signature, not decoration. Around 170 survive, and the genuine ones
share a secret: crucible steel centuries ahead of its time.

The metal was nearly pure — no slag, no shortcuts, with a carbon
content that matched 19th-century industrial iron. The design was
plain. No gilding, no ornament. It did one job better than anything
else on the field, and it outlasted its era.

That's the philosophy I hold and respect: **purist tools, efficient
systems, no compromise on quality**. No ornament where function will do.

- **Daily driver, barren by choice** — Linux, Vim/Neovim, ZSH. No
  framework, no plugin bloat.
- **Boring tools that outlast trends** — markdown, pandoc, make. The
  stack behind this site is the software equivalent of crucible steel:
  plain to look at, hard to break.
- **One job, done properly** — a build pipeline turns words into pages;
  a blade cuts. Neither one asks for applause.
- **Genuine over fashionable** — fake Ulfberhts flooded the market,
  ordinary iron stamped with a famous name. I'd rather carry a proven
  plain tool than a shiny counterfeit.

## Vartanovich

The second half of my handle is a **chess** tribute. **Tigran Vartanovich
Petrosian** — "Vartanovich" meaning son of Vartan — ruled the world
championship from 1963 to 1969. Everyone called him **Iron Tigran**,
and not for his biceps.

His style looked boring. Quiet moves, patient positioning, zero flash.
Opponents pushed and found nothing, then slowly lost to their own
mistakes. The chess writers sighed; the scoreboard didn't.

That's the same blade, sharpened differently. The Ulfberht outlasted
its era because its steel didn't break. Petrosian outlasted his because
his positions didn't. **Solidity over spectacle, patience over
brilliance, no compromise on quality.**

Boring wins championships. Boring doesn't break at 3am. I'll take
boring.
